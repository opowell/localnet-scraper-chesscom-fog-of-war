# Chess.com Fog of War scraper for Localnet

Reads `chess.com/variants/fog-of-war` pages for Localnet, a browser extension that is a personal search engine.
Localnet pulls it from here the first time it scrapes such a page, through the
[localnet-scrapers](https://github.com/opowell/localnet-scrapers) scraper source.

- `source.json`: what Localnet reads to know the scraper: its name, the URLs it reads, and the file to run; and `chess`, how chess.com draws and sounds its games, which Localnet's Read plays a game back with: the addresses of chess.com's own pieces (neo, alpha and classic), sounds and Fog of War fog. Localnet loads them from chess.com; neither it nor this repo keeps a copy.
- `chesscom-fog-of-war.js`: the parser. It is injected into the scraped page after Localnet's DOM helpers (content-scripts/utils/dom.js) and defines `window.__dynamicParser`.
- `chesscom-fog-of-war.test.ts`: its unit tests, run from a Localnet checkout (`pnpm parsers pull`, then `pnpm test parsers/localnet-scraper-chesscom-fog-of-war`).
- `saved-pages/` (not in git): real pages saved from the site, which Localnet's `e2e/saved-pages.test.ts` scrapes. See Localnet's README.

## What it reads

The **Chess.com Fog of War** parser (`content-scripts/parsers/chesscom-fog-of-war.js`, URL pattern `chess.com/variants/fog-of-war`) reads the site's Fog of War games and the pages that lead to them. The variants pages are an app that draws everything from what it is sent over its own connection (a WebSocket) and keeps it in its stores (Pinia's, on the page's Vue app, `#app.__vue_app__`), which only the page's own world can read, so the parser runs there (see Search flow); it asks the site nothing the page would not ask itself. The site writes its options into the path after an `&` (`/archive&p=ann&gameType=2pc&daysBack=0-9999`) and, once loaded, rewrites the address, dropping what it does not know and adding where in a game it is (`/game/112126760/20/1`), so the parser reads the address the page was loaded at (its navigation entry) and names the page's own as its `canonicalUrl`. The parser waits for what the site sends each page 9 s at most, all told, as a scrape has 15 s in all, the page's loading included; if it is not sent by then, the parser gives what it knows of the page (a listing, its title, its address) and why it failed (`failed`: `"chess.com did not send its Fog of War rapid leaderboard, showing numGamesTotal (20 leaders) in 9 s (the tab was hidden)"`), rather than leaving the scrape to time out with nothing to say why, or the page to be read as its text, which is only the site's menus. Its pages:
- **The leaderboards**, `/variants/fog-of-war/leaderboards`: a listing linking to each speed's leaderboard, `…/leaderboards&speed=<speed>` for each of the site's (blitz, rapid, bullet and hyper). The speed is the parser's own option: the page opens on a leaderboard of its own (games played), and the parser chooses the speed's tab as the page does (`selectType`), again if nothing has come of it in 3 s, then lists its 20 leaders, each linking to their games of that speed, the first most strongly (`1 / (1 + rank / 10)`).
- **A player's games**, `/variants/fog-of-war/archive&p=<player>&gameType=2pc&daysBack=0-9999`: a listing of their latest 20 two-player games, newest first (the page looks the player up by name and may list the players whose names begin so without choosing one, as a background tab in Firefox does; after 2.5 s the parser chooses the one of that very name, as pressing it would), linking to each by the rating of the weaker of its two players, so that a search prefers games between strong players: `2^{(r - 2000) / 200}` for a weaker player rated `r` (1 at 2000, twice that for every 200 points more, half for every 200 less; a player with no rating counts as a new one, at 1500), divided by `1 + rank / 10` as the newest come first. A game whose weaker player is rated 2400 is linked at 4 and one at 1600 at 0.25, sixteen times less, so the first is reached sooner and scores more wherever the two are on the list. With `&speed=<speed>`, only those of that speed, for which it takes more of the list as the page does when scrolled (`incrementPage`), 20 at a time, until it has 10, has looked through 100 or runs out of patience (below) (leaders often play one speed far more than another).
- **A game**, `/variants/fog-of-war/game/<number>`: titled `<white> vs <black>`, headed by a post's head naming both players (each linking to their games) and when it began, then the game as a chess block (see Item), read from the moves the site keeps (`pgn4`, written on a four-player board's squares, whose middle eight by eight is the board, `"h5-h7"` being `e2e4`) with when each was made and its player's clock after it, and nothing more: what is known of it besides is in its details, which Read shows in its side panel. Its details are its variant (Fog of War), speed, clock, the weaker player's rating (`minrating`, by which Results can be sorted and filtered, `minrating>=2200`), players, ratings, the ratings' changes (`rating changes`, `+5.8 · −6`), result, winner, ending, moves, minutes, mode (Rated or Casual) and when it was played. It links to its speed's leaderboard and to each player's games, so that saving it teaches a search what leads to games like it: the speed's leaderboard, and through it that speed's leaders' games of that speed, and the players' own games (see Learning). _Example:_ `"chess"` links to the leaderboards. Saving a rapid game found from it links `"chess"` to the rapid leaderboard at half the visit weight, learned, and to each player's games, and, where the rapid leaderboard has been scraped, to its leaders' rapid games at a quarter, so a search from `"chess"` reaches more rapid games, and more of those players'.
